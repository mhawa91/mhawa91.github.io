## Short answer
ERP and enterprise-application work here means helping a company decide which system fits, how the project is run, and who owns the data after go-live. I do not sell a license on this page. The work is selection, oversight, and governance.

## What you can expect
The usual situation is a mid-size company in Saudi Arabia or the GCC that is choosing a system, replacing one, or living with a project that has stalled. The output is a comparison and a written recommendation, then oversight if you want the work to continue.

## A practical sequence
### Start from the work, not the software
Write down the decisions the business must make: orders, stock, cash, projects, people. A system that does not match those decisions will not be saved by a longer feature list.

### Fix the process before the configuration
If the current process is unclear, configuring it into ERP only makes the confusion faster. Name the process owner, the exceptions, and the hand-offs first.

### Write requirements the vendors can answer
Requirements should say what the business must be able to do, including integrations, data, and the reports management actually uses. A demo of a standard screen is not a requirement.

### Decide data ownership before go-live
Customers, vendors, items, and accounts each need an owner in the business. Without that, reports diverge after launch.

### Plan the return path
Every go-live needs a way back if the first days fail: who decides, what data is restored, and how the business keeps operating.

## When a project is already in trouble
These are signs, not a score. Scope that keeps growing with no owner. Reports that do not match across teams. No named owner for master data. A vendor writing the requirements it will then deliver. No plan for the week after go-live. The response is to pause new build, review scope, data, and governance, and write what will be finished and what will change.
