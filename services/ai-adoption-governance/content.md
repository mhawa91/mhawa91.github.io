## Short answer
AI governance here is the rules for who may use AI tools, for what, with which data, and who reviews the result before it is relied on. It starts with three steps: list where AI tools are used today, decide which data may be entered, and write a one-page policy.

## Three steps
### 1. List current use
Ask each team which tools they already open: chat tools, copilots inside office software, and anything pasted into a browser. The list is more useful than a ban nobody follows.

### 2. Decide which data may be entered
Customer lists, contracts, national IDs, and unpublished financials are not the same as a public brochure. Classification should follow the company's own rules and the national references, including the Personal Data Protection Law and SDAIA's work on AI ethics. The authority's site is https://sdaia.gov.sa/.

### 3. Write one page
The page names the allowed uses, the data that must not be pasted, who reviews a result before it is sent to a customer or to management, and who to ask when a case is unclear. A policy people will not read is not a policy.

## What this is not
It is not a claim that a tool is "compliant" because a vendor said so. It is not a model-training project. And it is not the whole of IT management. Reporting and Power BI stay a supporting service under the wider technology work.

## What you leave with
A one-page use policy and a short rollout plan: who is told, what is stopped, and what is reviewed after the first month.

## A question the policy should answer
Who checks an AI result before the company relies on it? If the answer is "no one", the policy is not finished.
