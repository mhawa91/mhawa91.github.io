Vendor selection and RFPs

Choose the vendor with a document, not a demo

Short answer

Vendor selection here means writing what the company needs, asking vendors to answer the same questions, and comparing the answers in one table. You leave with a requirements document and an evaluation table. I do not publish prices, and I do not score vendors with invented weights.

What a requirements document should contain

Context

What the company does, why the system is being considered, and what decision the document supports.

Scope

What is in this phase, and what is explicitly later. A scope that includes everything is a scope that cannot be delivered.

Requirements by priority

What the business must be able to do, marked as required or later. Include the processes, not only a list of module names.

Data and integrations

Which systems stay, which data moves, and who owns each master-data type.

Security and compliance

Access, audit needs, and local obligations such as e-invoicing where they apply. Name the obligation. Do not paste a vendor's compliance claim as if it were yours.

Delivery and support

How the vendor implements, trains, and supports, and what happens in the first weeks after go-live.

How to respond

The same structure for every vendor, so the answers can sit in one table.

How to compare proposals

Read each answer against the requirement, not against the demo. Useful columns are: does it fit the process, what is the delivery risk, what integration is required, how is support provided, and what is commercial. The commercial column is a comparison, not a published price. Weights, if you use them, are your decision. I will not invent them.

Questions worth asking before you sign

Who configures, and who decides?

If the vendor both writes the requirements and marks them done, you have no independent view.

What is the data-migration plan?

Ask which records move, who cleans them, and what "done" means for a customer or an item.

What does the first month of support include?

Go-live week is not the end of the project. Ask who is available, and for how long.

What is outside the proposal?

Interfaces, historical data, training, and extra companies in the group are common gaps.

How do we leave?

If the project stops, what data can you take, and in what form?

What you leave with

A requirements document your team can stand behind, and a table that compares vendors on the same questions. The next step, if you want it, is help through the discussion and the service agreement.
